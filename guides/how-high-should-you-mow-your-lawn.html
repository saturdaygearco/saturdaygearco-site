How High Should You Mow Your Lawn?

The right answer depends on the grass, but one rule travels well: do not remove too much living leaf in one cut.

Quick answer: Set mowing height for your turf species and season, then mow often enough that you remove no more than about one-third of the leaf tissue at a time. Avoid scalping and keep the blade sharp.

The one-third rule

University turf guidance consistently warns against removing a large portion of the grass blade in one mowing. The University of Minnesota recommends removing no more than one-third of leaf tissue. Penn State similarly advises mowing when new turf is no more than one-third higher than the desired cutting height. The point is plant stress: an occasional missed week should not turn the next mowing into a scalp job.

There is no universal perfect height

Different turf species tolerate different mowing heights, and regional recommendations matter. A height that works for a cool-season northern lawn should not automatically be copied onto warm-season turf. Identify the grass first, then use local extension guidance for its recommended range. During stressful heat or drought, mowing at the higher end of an appropriate range can leave more leaf area on the plant.

Sharp blades are not optional

A dull blade tears instead of cutting cleanly. Ragged tips make a freshly mowed lawn look rough and are a sign that the blade needs attention. Inspect the cutting edge periodically and service it according to the mower manufacturer's instructions. If you sharpen it yourself, reinstall it correctly and maintain blade balance.

Do you need to bag clippings?

Usually not. When mowing frequently enough that clippings are short, they can be left on the lawn to decompose. University of Minnesota Extension notes that decomposed clippings contribute nutrients and improve soil quality. If growth has become so tall that mowing leaves heavy clumps, spread or remove those clumps rather than smothering the turf.

Change your mowing pattern

Alternating direction helps prevent repeatedly pushing grass the same way and can reduce visible wheel patterns. On slopes, follow the mower manufacturer's safety instructions; safe technique differs between walk-behind and riding equipment. Never sacrifice stability just to create stripes.

Bottom line

Healthy mowing is less about chasing one magic deck setting and more about matching height to the grass, mowing before it becomes overgrown, and making a clean cut. If you are constantly removing half the blade, the problem is frequency—not the mower.
