Quick answer: Do not buy by peak wattage alone. List the equipment that must run at the same time, total its running load, account for the largest relevant motor/compressor startup surge, then choose enough capacity to avoid operating at the edge of the generator's rating.

The four numbers and decisions that matter

Running watts

Add the continuous wattage of equipment you expect to operate simultaneously. This is the baseline load the generator must sustain.

Starting watts

Refrigerators, pumps, compressors and other motor loads can briefly demand more power when starting. Verify equipment labels or manuals instead of assuming the running number covers the surge.

Essential loads

A refrigerator, lights and internet are a different job from central air, electric water heating and a well pump. Decide what truly needs power before shopping.

Connection method

Extension-cord loads and home circuits are different setups. For home circuits or hardwired equipment, use a properly designed transfer-switch or approved interlock arrangement installed as required.

Carbon-monoxide safety: The CDC says to run a portable generator outdoors more than 20 feet from windows, doors and vents. Never operate one in a house or garage, even with doors open. Use battery-powered or battery-backup CO detectors in the home, and follow the manufacturer's placement instructions.

Build your outage plan before you buy

Write down each essential appliance or circuit and find its rated electrical demand. Avoid treating generic online wattage charts as exact specifications; two appliances that do the same job can have different loads. Your own equipment label or manual is the better source.

Basic essentials

Refrigerator/freezer, selected lighting, modem/router, phone charging and a few outlets can often be managed by deliberately staggering loads.

Motor-heavy loads

Well pumps, air conditioners and compressors deserve extra attention because starting demand can materially change the required generator capacity.

Fuel and runtime

More output generally brings more fuel use, weight and noise. Capacity you will never use can become an expensive burden during storage and refueling.

CO shutoff technology

Automatic CO shutoff is a worthwhile safety feature, but it does not make indoor, garage or near-home operation safe. Correct outdoor placement remains essential.

Portable generator or power station?

If your needs are smaller, quiet battery operation near the appliance may matter more than sustained high output. Fuel generators remain useful when long-duration power and higher loads are the priority, provided fuel storage and safe outdoor operation are practical.

Our practical sizing rule

Buy for the load plan, not the biggest number on the box. Total what will actually run together, verify startup demand, leave sensible operating headroom and make sure the connection method is appropriate. If the plan involves energizing home circuits, have a qualified electrician handle the connection design or installation.
