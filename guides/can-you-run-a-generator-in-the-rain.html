Can You Run a Portable Generator in the Rain?

Plan for both electrical shock and carbon monoxide. Solving one hazard must not create the other.

By Nicholas · Published September 28, 2026

Quick answer: Do not run an ordinary portable generator exposed to rain, standing water or water spray. Follow the exact model's manual. If its instructions permit a running cover or other wet-weather setup, use only a compatible product exactly as directed. Never move the generator into a garage, shed, porch, carport or other enclosed or partly enclosed space to keep it dry.

Why “just put it under something” is not enough

Wet generator components and connections can create a shock or electrocution hazard. At the same time, moving an engine closer to the house or enclosing it can allow deadly carbon monoxide to collect. The safe plan has to satisfy the generator manufacturer's rain instructions and keep exhaust safely away from people and buildings.

The Consumer Product Safety Commission says most consumer portable generators are not weatherproof and tells owners to follow the manufacturer's wet-weather instructions. Westinghouse manuals warn against rain, snow, water spray, damp locations and standing water. A generic tarp, improvised box or patio roof should not be treated as automatic permission to run a particular machine.

Four checks before wet-weather operation

  1. Read the exact manual. Find the weather, placement, grounding and extension-cord instructions for the model number on your generator.
  2. Use only a permitted weather setup. If the manufacturer allows a purpose-built running cover, confirm that it fits the exact model and install it as directed. A storage cover is not necessarily a running cover.
  3. Keep the site dry and stable. Do not operate in standing water, where runoff can reach the machine, or where wet hands are needed to handle plugs.
  4. Preserve safe exhaust clearance. The CDC says generators belong outdoors, more than 20 feet from windows, doors and vents, with working battery-powered or battery-backup carbon-monoxide detectors in the home.
Do not use these rain “solutions”:
  • A garage with the door open
  • A porch, carport, basement, crawlspace or shed
  • A loose tarp that can block cooling or touch hot parts
  • An improvised enclosure that traps exhaust
  • Indoor operation because the generator has automatic CO shutoff

Automatic CO shutoff is an added safety feature, not permission to operate near or inside a building.

Plan the cords and load before the storm

Use outdoor-rated cords sized for the connected load and length required by the manual. Inspect cords and plugs, keep connections out of water, and do not overload a cord, outlet or generator circuit. Never backfeed a household receptacle. Powering home circuits requires properly designed transfer equipment installed as required.

Do a dry-weather practice setup before an outage: choose the location, measure the distance from openings, confirm the permitted rain protection, and verify that cords reach without creating a trip or water hazard. If conditions cannot meet the instructions, wait or use a different power plan.

When a battery power station is the better fit

A portable power station has no engine exhaust, so it can run suitable loads indoors. It still must be kept dry and used within its output, charging and temperature limits.
