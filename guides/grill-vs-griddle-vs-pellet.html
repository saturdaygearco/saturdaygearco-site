Gas Grill vs. Griddle vs. Pellet Grill

Three good ways to cook outside—and three very different reasons to own one.

Quick answer: Gas is the straightforward all-rounder, a griddle wins on flat-top foods and breakfast, and pellet cooking is strongest when smoke flavor and temperature-controlled low-and-slow cooking matter most.

Gas grill: fast and familiar

Gas grills heat quickly, give direct access to open-grate cooking and make weeknight burgers, chicken, vegetables and steaks straightforward. They are a strong choice when convenience and traditional grilling are the priority.

Griddle: maximum surface contact

A griddle replaces open grates with a flat cooking surface. That makes it especially useful for smash burgers, bacon, eggs, pancakes, chopped vegetables and other small foods that can fall through grill grates. The tradeoff is that grease management and surface care become part of the routine.

Pellet grill: smoke and temperature control

Pellet cookers feed wood pellets into a fire pot and electronically manage temperature. Their appeal is controlled smoking, roasting and longer cooks with wood-fired flavor. Some current designs can also reach higher temperatures for searing, but buyers should compare the specific model rather than assume every pellet cooker behaves like a gas grill.

Which fits Saturday?

Choose gas if you want to walk outside and cook dinner with minimal planning. Choose a griddle if breakfast, smash burgers, fajitas and high-volume flat-top cooking dominate your menu. Choose pellet if barbecue, ribs, brisket and smoke flavor are the reason you're cooking outdoors in the first place.

Bottom line

There is no universal winner because the cooking surfaces solve different problems. The best purchase is the one that matches what you actually cook most often—not the longest feature list.
