Quick answer: Choose by the mess you clean most often. Small portable vacs excel at cars and quick cleanup, mid-size wheeled vacs are the general-garage sweet spot, and large-capacity machines make sense for bulky debris and liquid pickup. Then compare airflow, hose diameter, filtration and portability—not tank gallons alone.

Shop vac sizing in 30 seconds

Cars & quick cleanup

Prioritize: portability, compact storage and useful detailing attachments.

A smaller tank is easier to carry and is usually enough when the debris volume is low.

General garage

Prioritize: balanced capacity, reach and maneuverability.

A mid-size wheeled wet/dry vac is often the most versatile one-machine solution.

Wood chips & bulky debris

Prioritize: hose diameter, capacity and easy emptying.

Larger debris is where a restrictive hose and small tank become annoying quickly.

Renovation & fine dust

Prioritize: appropriate filtration and dust-management features.

For demanding fine-dust control, consider whether a purpose-built dust extractor better matches the job.

Tank size is about workflow

A larger tank means fewer stops to empty debris or liquid, not automatically stronger suction. A compact vacuum can be easier to carry into a vehicle, onto a workbench or around the house. Large 12- to 16-gallon machines are better suited to bigger cleanup jobs where capacity and long cords matter more than portability.

Look past peak horsepower

Peak horsepower is widely used on consumer wet/dry vacuums, but it is not operational horsepower. DEWALT notes that the rating includes laboratory motor-output conditions and that motors do not operate at that peak in actual use. When comparable data are available, airflow in CFM and suction or water-lift specifications provide additional useful context.

Hose diameter changes what fits

A smaller hose is easier to maneuver and works well for dust, vehicle interiors and tight spaces. A larger hose is less likely to clog on wood chips and bulky debris. Before buying, think about what you actually vacuum most often and whether your preferred nozzles, dust separators or power-tool adapters fit the hose system.

Filtration matters

Fine dust is a different job from picking up screws or water. Match the filter and bag to the debris and follow the vacuum manufacturer's wet-pickup instructions before collecting liquid. For serious dust control, a purpose-built dust extractor can offer features such as automatic filter cleaning, tool activation and specified HEPA filtration that a basic cleanup vacuum may not.

Cordless or corded?

Cordless wins for fast cleanup and mobility, especially if you already own batteries in the same platform. Corded machines avoid battery-runtime limits and are usually the better fit for long sessions. Battery and charger inclusion also matters to real purchase price because many cordless vacuums are sold as bare tools.

Features worth paying attention to

Look beyond headline capacity. A drain can make liquid cleanup easier on larger tanks. Onboard hose and accessory storage reduces the odds of losing attachments. Cord length and hose reach determine how often you move the machine. Noise matters in an attached garage. If you intend to connect the vacuum to power tools, verify hose and adapter compatibility before buying.

Our practical sizing rule

Buy the smallest machine that comfortably handles your normal mess, not the largest tank that fits your budget. For detailing and quick cleanup, portability is king. For a general garage, a mid-size wheeled wet/dry vacuum balances storage, reach and capacity. For renovation debris and large liquid pickup, move up in tank size and prioritize a drain, sturdy wheels and a hose that will not choke on debris.

The shop-vac buying shortlist

These are use-case recommendations based on the buying criteria above, not hands-on product tests. Check the current manufacturer specifications and the exact model before ordering.

Buying path

Compact detailer

For car interiors and small cleanup, compare carry weight, hose reach, crevice tools and storage. A modest tank is fine when you empty it after each job.

Watch out: Small hoses can clog on larger debris.

Buying path

Mid-size garage wet/dry vac

For a one-machine garage setup, prioritize maneuverability, replacement filters, cord and hose reach, and a tank you can empty comfortably.

Watch out: A larger tank adds bulk without improving suction by itself.

Buying path

Dust-focused extractor

For frequent tool-connected fine dust, compare specified filtration, bag availability, hose adapter fit and automatic tool start where useful.

Watch out: Check job-specific dust rules and filtration requirements before relying on a general-purpose vacuum.
