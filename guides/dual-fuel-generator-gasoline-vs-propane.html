Dual-Fuel Generator: Gasoline vs. Propane Watts

The fuel you plan to use changes the output number you should shop by.

By Nicholas · Published September 26, 2026

Quick answer: Read both sets of ratings on a dual-fuel generator. If propane is your outage fuel, size the load against its propane running watts and check its propane starting watts for motors. The gasoline number on the front of the box can overstate the capacity you will have on propane. Fuel flexibility is useful only when the lower-rated mode still runs your essentials.

A real example: the same generator, two ratings

Westinghouse lists the WGen9500DF at 9,500 running and 12,500 peak watts on gasoline, versus 8,500 running and 11,200 peak watts on propane. That is a 1,000-watt difference in continuous output for this particular model. Do not apply that difference to another machine; read its own fuel-specific label and manual. The numbers are manufacturer ratings, not our independent test.

How to size your propane load

  1. List the equipment that must run at the same time and get its actual rated demand from labels or manuals.
  2. Add the continuous watts and compare that total with the propane running rating, leaving practical headroom.
  3. Identify compressors, pumps and other motors that may start while those loads are running. Check that the relevant starting demand fits within the propane peak rating and the generator manual's instructions.
  4. Decide how each load connects. A household circuit needs appropriate transfer equipment; never backfeed an outlet.

Which fuel is more practical to keep ready?

Gasoline plan

Use the gasoline rating when you know gasoline will be available and can follow the manual's storage, stabilization and maintenance directions. Plan for refueling and safe storage. Do not assume a long runtime figure applies at your expected load.

Propane plan

Use the propane rating and confirm the tank, regulator and hose setup specified by the generator maker. Propane avoids storing gasoline in the generator's tank, but the fuel supply and temperature can affect practical operation. Check the exact manual and your planned cylinder arrangement before buying.

What dual fuel does not solve

Fuel choice does not make a portable generator safe indoors. The CDC says to operate it outdoors more than 20 feet from windows, doors and vents, never in a house or garage even with doors open, and to use battery-powered or battery-backup CO detectors in the home. Follow the generator's operating and placement instructions too. For quiet indoor power without an engine exhaust, compare a battery power station after checking its output and watt-hours; a battery has a different runtime limit.

Before you buy

  • Verify both fuels' running and starting watts for the exact model revision.
  • Check fuel-specific runtime at a stated load; it is not a promise for your own load or tank.
  • Confirm outlets, breaker limits and the connection method for your equipment.
  • Plan transport, outdoor placement, maintenance, fuel and safe storage.
